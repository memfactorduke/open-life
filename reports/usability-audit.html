Principles used to judge the flows

These are the rules from the current Apple Human Interface Guidelines pages, Nielsen Norman Group, and the W3C forms guidance. They are applied to Mem's Life as it is specified: Tasks, Workouts, and a photo-and-text food diary. No new product area is recommended.

  1. Start in the work. Apple's onboarding page says people learn by doing, a tutorial should be short and optional, and setup that is not required waits. A permission belongs at first launch only when the app cannot function without it.
  2. Show the frequent choice first. Apple's layout page and Nielsen's progressive-disclosure article say the first screen should hold what people use often, and the control that opens the rest should say what is behind it.
  3. Let people recognize the action. Budiu's recognition article says unlabeled icons, memorized gestures, and a general tutorial ask for recall. Apple's writing and buttons pages want a verb, one or two prominent buttons, and a destructive action that is not the primary button.
  4. One job on a narrow screen. The same jobs when the space grows. Layout follows the available space. A tab bar navigates and stays visible. A sidebar is for a wide window. Apple's tab-bar page says to avoid a More tab and not to hide tabs.
  5. Plain words, and an honest state. WCAG 2.2 success criteria 3.3.1, 3.3.2, and 3.3.3 require a visible label, an error in text, and a known correction. Too much instruction is as harmful as too little. Placeholder text is not a label.
  6. Ask for access when it pays off. Apple's managing-accounts page says to require an account only when the product needs one, to name the real sign-in method, and to make deletion findable. Camera, notifications, and WHOOP are not that account.

Before the first flows

Seen The baseline phone captures, dated in the page as Tuesday, October 13, were a marketing shell around a thin daily view.

Sign in

The phone width opened with "A private place for everyday life," the headline "Make room for what matters," and a second headline "Sign in to your space." Email and password were labeled, which was already right. Under the button, the page taught the local setup command. That is operator work on a person's first screen.

Today

The tab bar sat at the top. The page said "Your daily focus," greeted "Fictional Release Reviewer," and offered Capture a task and Log food. The rest of the screen was three cards with titles that did not name a state: "A clear place to start," "Room for movement," and "Your diary is ready for your first entry." There was no Start workout on that capture. A footer repeated "Your life, in your own space."

Settings

"Make yourself at home" sat above every preference: display name, time zone, units, and appearance, then "Opening view…" twice, then sign-out. Notifications, agent credentials, and WHOOP were not a separate step on the phone capture. On the desktop baseline text they were further down the same page, including the MCP endpoint and the bearer-header instructions.

Workouts, Tasks, and Food

Workouts showed three peer controls, Library, Train, and WHOOP, and then stopped on "Opening view…". Tasks and Food also stopped on "Opening view…". Those three captures establish the chrome and the workout tabs. They do not establish the old task editor or the old food form, because the features had not finished loading.

Tablet and desktop baselines used the same pages with a sidebar. The sidebar items carried a second line: "Your daily focus," "Plans & progress," "Training & history," "Your food diary," and "Make it yours," plus the brand line "A little more intentional."

Narrow web windows keep five tabs: Today, Tasks, Workouts, Food, Settings. A wide window uses the same five as a sidebar. That is the same information architecture with a different amount on screen, which is what the layout guidance asks for. Five is the upper end of Apple's "five or fewer" note for a customizable iPad tab bar. Settings is a place, not an action, so it belongs in the bar rather than disguised as a button.

Native source follows the same split, with two differences that the web screenshots do not show. Sign-in starts at "Connect your space" when the phone does not already know the installation address, then continues to email and password. Settings adds "Sync & recovery." A wide native window, at least 1024 points and not at a large text size, uses a sidebar; otherwise it uses the bottom bar.

Flows

Sign in

Seen Web, phone width, after capture and apps/web/src/App.tsx.

The only fields are Email address and Password. The button says "Sign in," and "Signing in…" while it works. A rejected password says "The email or password was not accepted. Check your details and try again." A missed service says to check the connection. An ended session says the person can sign in again and that unsaved preferences are still there. "Need access?" explains that the installation owner handles recovery. It does not lead with a setup command.

Native apps/mobile/src/App.tsx keeps the address step only when no valid installation URL is stored. Continue checks the space, then the form is email and password. Change space clears the password. That extra step is the right alternative on a device that is not already opened at the server. It should not be copied back onto the website.

Today

Seen Phone and desktop after captures, and both Today components.

The empty day no longer explains the product. It offers the three captures and says "Nothing needs your attention" and "No entries yet." When there is an active session, Resume workout replaces Start workout and the row shows how many sets are recorded. Needs-you rows use words such as "Due now," "Ready for you," and "Outcome needs checking," plus a sentence for the waiting reason. Waiting items sit in a disclosure. Food shows at most three entries and then "View food diary."

A failed refresh keeps the last confirmed view and says it may have changed. On the phone, an offline attention cache says "Device view · server freshness unverified." That is the honest state. It does not pretend the server has the latest day.

Tasks

Seen in apps/web/src/features/Tasks.tsx and Productivity.tsx. Pending after screenshot. The shell capture set has no tasks-after image, and this report did not open the scratch file named in the task receipt.

Opening a task shows its phase and the next required step. One button changes with the state: Release to Next, Continue work, Confirm final review, or Complete task. Edit task is secondary. Under Release to Next, one sentence says that eligible agent steps can start and human steps still wait. Planned work says to release it before any step can start. Complete stays unavailable until required steps and a required final review are satisfied.

The receipt describes the earlier screen as a broad editor with organization, agent setup, and history around the routine work, and the current screen as reading first. The source matches that description. Completed, archived, configuration, and recurrence are not the first controls. Agent setup is not on the reading view.

A step card still prints the raw responsibility value, such as "agent," beside Required or Optional. That is leftover jargon on a secondary card, not on the first button.

Workouts

Seen Phone after capture and apps/web/src/features/Sessions.tsx.

WHOOP is gone from this screen. The choice is Workout or Routines. Before history has loaded, Load workouts is a full-width button and Start workout sits in a quieter form whose routine defaults to "Empty workout." Name workout is disclosed. The loaded logging screen, which this capture did not show, uses Log set as the button label in source, and Save correction when a set is being edited.

Load workouts is an acceptable control for a failed or deferred fetch. It is a poor primary button for an ordinary visit. The likely action is to start or resume. The alternative is to load on arrival and keep Start workout prominent, with an error and a retry if the load fails.

Food

Seen in apps/web/src/features/Food.tsx. Pending after screenshot in this shell set.

The editor asks "What did you eat?" and offers Add photo. The save button says "Save entry." There is no calorie or nutrient field in that form. Opening another entry while a draft is open says to save or discard the current draft first. That matches the diary in the spec and does not add nutrition tracking.

Settings and agents

Seen Phone Settings and Connections captures, plus web and native settings and AgentAccess.tsx.

Profile holds the name, time zone, units, and appearance. Notifications are their own row, so the system permission is not asked on the way into the app. Account shows the email and signs out. Connections holds agent access and WHOOP.

Agent access opens on "Choose what an agent can read or change," the issued list, and Add agent. Creating a key asks for a name and a purpose, and the purpose fills the scopes. The secret screen says the key is shown once. Copy key, Done · hide key, and Manual setup details come after that. The bearer-header sentence is inside the disclosure, not on the settings index. Revoke is on the grant and says revoking ends that agent's access. A revoked grant keeps the history line.

WHOOP's current source says "Not set up" when the installation has no connection, "No account connected," and "WHOOP cannot connect until it is set up for this installation." Disconnect asks for confirmation and says local workouts and saved observations remain. Deleting the cache is a second confirmation and also says local workouts remain. The connections screenshot still shows the older line "unconfigured · observations are stale." That line is not in the current status code. "Last successful refresh: Never" and "View saved observations (0)" are still in the source. The zero is a count of saved rows, but beside an unconnected account it can be read as a measurement. "No saved observations" would be harder to misread.

Consequential guards that are still in the source

The redesign was not allowed to make a dangerous action easier by hiding its confirmation. These checks are still in the files named above.

  • A Planned task cannot start its steps until it is released to Next. Releasing does not mark a human step done.
  • Complete task is disabled until the step state says the required work is done. A required final review is a separate Confirm final review action. Reopen final review remains available.
  • Switching tasks with an unsaved draft stops and asks the person to keep editing or discard the draft. An uncertain save has to be resolved before that switch. The button is Retry the same change.
  • On the web, Sign out stays disabled while preference edits are unsaved. Discard changes is separate. Native sign-out, when local changes are waiting, offers Keep editing, Retain and sign out, Discard and sign out, and Export and sign out. Discard is the destructive choice.
  • An agent secret cannot be retrieved again after it is hidden. Revoke does not delete the grant's history.
  • WHOOP disconnect and cache deletion do not delete locally recorded workouts. The confirmations say so.
  • A phone offline Today view says the server freshness is unverified. It does not present the device cache as the server's copy.
  • A food draft blocks opening a different entry until it is saved or discarded.

Web sign-out is not the native retain-or-export flow. The web app is online-first and does not claim a durable offline outbox. Those two guards should stay different.

Alternatives that were set aside

Alternative Why it lost
A first-run story that teaches phases, agents, and permissions. Apple says teaching too much is forgotten. The recognition article says a general tutorial is not rehearsed. The private account is the only gate, and on the web it is one form.
Permanent captions under the tabs and under ordinary buttons. The old sidebar lines described the sections without adding an action. WCAG 3.3.2 warns that too much instruction is harmful. The captions are no longer rendered.
WHOOP as a third workout mode. It is a connection, not a way to log a set. Putting it beside Train made a provider look required. It now sits with agent access.
One long settings page. Preferences, alerts, credentials, and sign-out are different jobs. The index discloses one at a time. Nielsen's criterion is that the first list contain the frequent choices and that the next level be obvious. Profile, Notifications, Connections, and Account do that.
A phone sidebar, or a different structure on the web. The tab-bar guidance prefers a tab bar when space is tight and a sidebar when the structure needs room. The five destinations stay the same.
Opening every task in the full editor. The frequent choice is the next state change. Organization, history, and agent setup are still reachable, but they are not the first screen.

Source fixes have regression coverage and passed their corrected immutable native build checks. The formatter failure matches the documented Hermes/polyfill issue; direct native date formatting preserves the diary day. The complete browser run found one further defect: Today chose the oldest three meals. Both clients now select the newest three independent of response ordering.

The corrected native Release build has proved the main redesigned flows on iPhone and iPad simulators. Local alert testing found that cold offline pause/finish can leave a scheduled alert; cancellation and denied-permission recovery passed regression tests, independent review, and actual OS acceptance. A final timer display refinement distinguishes verified expiry from unknown time, discloses the retained offline timer, and stops idle ticking. Its expanded 156-case browser suite and actual final native presentation checks pass. After the time gate, the signed parallel test app installed and launched on the physical iPad. Its landscape sign-in screen is readable. XCTest could not enable automation; Apple screen sharing is unavailable on this iPadOS 26.6.1 device. Camera, offline capture, and app-specific Settings checks await a one-time device setting check. Personal data remains preserved. Live WHOOP consent requires a configured account; remote push is currently disabled. Deployment and Apple distribution remain separate acceptance steps.
